# MW-Gold — the Sanskrit twin of LSJ-Gold and LS-Gold (v0, 2026-11-09)

Lemmatizer accuracy on the **dictionary-cited stratum of Sanskrit**: the words Monier-Williams' *Sanskrit-English Dictionary* (1899) cites for a headword, relocated into the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit (DCS) and validated by the DCS annotators' lemma or by an independent Pāṇinian word generator. Same question as LSJ-Gold (`../lsjgold/`) and LS-Gold (`../lsgold/`), same protocol, same honesty rules (report, never patch; pre-register before scoring; every figure traces to a row; negative results ship). The feasibility note written before any relocation, with its predictions, is `../../sanskrit_feasibility/REPORT.md`; the abbreviation-to-edition map is `../../sanskrit_feasibility/mw_abbrev_map.json`.

**Read this first.** The only large human-lemmatized Sanskrit corpus is the DCS, and the DCS is also the training data of the strongest Sanskrit systems. Most of this gold is "the DCS annotators' lemma, where it equals Monier-Williams' headword". A system trained on the DCS is therefore scored on its own training distribution, and ByT5-Sanskrit partly on sentences it was trained on. Those figures measure consistency with the DCS annotation tradition on the words a lexicographer chose to cite, not generalisation. The independent parts are the `vidyut` route and the systems not trained on DCS.

## How a locus is made (`pipeline/mwgold/`)
1. `mw_parse.py` extracts (headword = Cologne `k1` in IAST; MW's member hyphenation from `k2`; abbreviation; locus parts with Roman numerals converted) for every `<ls>` with a numeric locus, including the continued form `<ls n="Mn. viii,">52</ls>`.
2. `relocate_mw.py` maps an in-scope abbreviation's locus to a reference (`RV. i, 32, 5` → hymn 1.32 verse 5; `Kathās. lii, 211` → taraṅga 52 counted through the work → lambaka 9, taraṅga 1 by GRETIL's lambaka sizes; `Megh. 108` → uttaramegha verse by the pūrvamegha's length; TS, MS, KS, AB, PB, Kauśika and Gautama by the DCS chapter, which is MW's cited unit: window = chapter, flagged), takes the sentences of the cited verse ± 1 verse, and validates each DCS word: **dcs** (DCS lemma key = headword key), **dcs_cpd** (2–6 consecutive compound members, including the negative particle a/an-, whose lemmas join with vowel sandhi to the headword, or that match MW's own member list), **dcs_tmesis** (a Vedic verb whose bare root plus a separate nearby preverb gives MW's compound verb), **vidyut** (the DCS lemma differs but shares three letters and vidyut-kosha's analyses of the word contain the headword) and **surface** (the word itself is the headword, as for MW's adverb entries; DCS lemma shares three letters). Exactly one candidate in the window → a gold locus; several → the cited verse decides when it holds exactly one (546 times), else ambiguous; none → not located; verse not in the aligned edition → locator_not_in_edition.
3. `skt_caches.py` runs each system on the DCS words of each sentence, pre-segmented; `score_mw.py` compares; `leaderboard_mw.py` renders `docs/mw-gold/`.
4. `validation_sheet_mw.py` / `validation_score_mw.py`: the blind relocation check (60 random + 10 anchors + 10 plants, key private until scored).

Comparison key (`sktnorm.key`): NFC, lower case, Vedic accents and avagraha off, anusvāra and class nasals before a stop folded to ṃ, Vedic ḻ → ḍ, homonym digits off. Vowel length is kept. Compound loci are scored on the system's lemmas for the span joined with vowel sandhi (or on a single lemma for the first member that already equals the headword: a system that keeps the compound whole is right).

## Results: relocation (`relocation_stats.json`)
Of 27,760 MW citations into the 32 texts in scope (1,189 with a locus of the wrong shape for the text are not counted), **12,946 located** (46.6%); ambiguous 446; not located 11,413; locator not in our edition 2,955. Without the Aṣṭādhyāyī stratum: 11,618 of 19,289 (60.2%). Routes: dcs 8,826 · dcs_cpd 2,776 · dcs_tmesis 584 · vidyut 527 · surface 233. Window: verse 12,542, chapter 404. **9,304 distinct headwords (10,246 MW entries)** over 32 texts: Ṛgveda 5,646, Manusmṛti 2,579, Atharvaveda 1,518, Aṣṭādhyāyī 1,328 (its own stratum: a word inside a sūtra), Bhāgavatapurāṇa 368, Kathāsaritsāgara 321, Kumārasambhava 191, Aitareyabrāhmaṇa 147, Kirātārjunīya 107, then 23 texts under 100. By how often MW cites the entry with a locus: once 6,970, 2–5 times 4,725, 6+ 1,251; MW cites far more entries once than the Greek and Latin lexica do, so the cited-once stratum is half the gold.

Not located, read by hand on the Ṛgveda and Manu: (a) roots and compound verbs whose finite forms carry another preverb or none in the cited verse (`dā`, `dhā`, `kṛ`, `gam`, `grah` top the list); (b) DCS lemmatizes participles and nouns of action to the root (`jāta` → jan, `gata` → gam) where MW enters the derivative; (c) MW's homonymous and class-10 headwords vs DCS's `-ay` stems; (d) verse drift where MW's edition numbers differently (Bhāgavata: 955 citations point at verses the GRETIL/Bombay text numbers otherwise or that DCS does not hold; Kathāsaritsāgara 298; Śatapatha 414 where the VPC is not yet sentence-split).

**Pre-registered predictions** (`REPORT.md` §7) against the outcome: alignment ≥ 90% on Manu, RV, BhP, Kum, Kir — met (92.6–100%); located 45–65% of the non-Pāṇini citations — met (60.2%), 8,500–12,400 loci — met (11,618); Pāṇini ≤ 10% — **missed** (15.7%: more sūtras name the word they teach than predicted); compound loci 25–40% of the gold — **missed** (21.4%); vidyut route < 10% — met (4.1%); blind precision ≥ 93% — met (below).

## Results: blind relocation check (`validation_final/`, `validation/`)
**Sheet 2 (the gold as published, seed 20261110)**: 60 random loci + 10 anchors + 10 plants; readers **opus-5-5 and sonnet-5-5** as blind subagents with nothing but the sheet (a planned third reader, fable-5-1, stopped before finishing the run, so opus and sonnet are the gate readers). Both passed the gate (anchors 10/10, plants 10/10 each). Random loci: opus yes 59, no 0, unsure 1; sonnet yes 58, no 0, unsure 2; **relocation precision 100%** (Wilson 93.8–100) for both; both yes on 57/60 (95.0% [86.3–98.3]); κ −0.02 (undefined in practice: neither reader said no, and the three disagreements are one reader's "unsure", S028 *turi*, S043 *kāpā*, S047 *āpi*). The fable reader's answers were complete on disk before it stopped and are scored as a third, non-gate reader: gate 10/10 and 10/10, yes 59, **no 1**: S047, where MW's headword was shown as *ā-pi* and the token is the noun *āpi* "friend": the gold is right about the word and the sheet's hyphenation (from MW's `k2`) suggested the verb entry. Key and verdicts published after scoring.
**Sheet 1 (the 12,370-locus gold before the tmesis route, seed 20261109)** is kept as a second check: opus 57 yes / **3 no** / 0 unsure, sonnet 57 / 0 / 3, fable 60 / 0 / 0; all gates passed; κ (opus, sonnet) 0.15. Opus's three rejections are the weak routes: *ghoṣi* (surface route; opus reads an aorist passive of ghuṣ), *hiṃsrā* (surface; the feminine adjective, not MW's noun *hiṃsrā*) and *devatyā* (vidyut; the feminine of the adjective *devatya*). These are the cost of MW's habit of entering a feminine or a form as its own headword: reported, not removed.

Strict (nasals as printed) is within 0.15 points of blind for every row. By text (≥ 100 loci), ByT5 runs from 76.8 (Kumārasambhava) to 89.1 (Bhāgavatapurāṇa), Ṛgveda 80.4, Manu 86.7, Atharvaveda 82.0, Aṣṭādhyāyī 82.2; Stanza from 56.5 (Kumārasambhava) to 79.6 (Aitareyabrāhmaṇa), Ṛgveda 67.1 although its training treebank is drawn from the Ṛgveda. By route, ByT5 is 96.4% on the dcs route (n 8,747), 77.7% on compound spans, **0% on the 584 tmesis loci** (it prints the bare root, as DCS does), 10.2% on vidyut and 5.2% on surface; the baseline row is the generator behind the vidyut route and scores 54.3% there, which is its circular share.

**Reading.** (1) On the 89% of loci where the gold is the DCS lemma, ByT5 reproduces it 91.9% of the time: it has learned the DCS convention, as it was trained to. (2) On the 11% where Monier-Williams' headword differs from the DCS lemma (tmesis compound verbs, MW's participles and feminines entered as headwords, adverbs in -am), every DCS-trained system falls to 5–21%: these are convention differences, not misreadings, and the benchmark counts them against the system exactly as LSJ-Gold and LS-Gold do; they are reported as their own column so a reader can drop them. (3) **The rare-word cliff of the Greek and Latin tables does not appear for ByT5 or Stanza**: cited-once 82.5 vs cited 6+ 85.3 (ByT5), 68.8 vs 71.2 (Stanza). It does appear for the lexicon-driven vidyut-cheda (36.5 vs 63.8). The likely reason is the circularity: the DCS-trained systems have seen these very tokens annotated. (4) vidyut-cheda and the baseline lemmatize compound members and derived nouns to roots (vacana → vac) and lose most compound and derivative loci on that convention alone; vidyut-cheda also returned no analysis for 8,636 sentences (mostly Vedic forms), which are its 1,423 missing loci.

**Pre-registered predictions for the systems** (`REPORT.md` §7.4) against the outcome: ByT5 ≥ 95% blind — **missed** (82.8; 96.4 on the dcs route alone); Stanza 70–85% overall — **missed** narrowly (69.5); Stanza ≥ 90% on the Ṛgveda — **missed** (67.1); vidyut-cheda 60–80% — **missed** (41.9); every system lower on compound spans — met; cited-once drop of 10–25 points for the trained systems — **missed** (ByT5 −2.8, Stanza −2.4).

**Run notes.** ByT5 was run only on the 10,561 sentences that hold a gold locus (a 582M-parameter model on CPU), in bfloat16 because the float32 load failed on this machine's commit memory; greedy decoding in bf16 can differ from float32 on rare outputs. 91 ByT5 loci are missing (sentences whose lemma count did not match the word count). The first vidyut-cheda cache mapped its re-segmented tokens back by substring search and misplaced lemmas; it was discarded unscored and rebuilt with a character alignment and an ASCII-safe SLP1 input (Vedic ṁ and ḻ raised errors). The scorer first applied the nasal fold before joining compound members (*sam* + *pad* ≠ *saṃpad*); fixed before these figures, a change of 0 to +0.16 points per figure. No system output was edited.

## Honesty
- **Circularity.** Routes dcs, dcs_cpd and dcs_tmesis (93% of the loci) take the DCS lemma as the oracle. ByT5-Sanskrit was fine-tuned on the DCS; Stanza-sa on UD_Sanskrit-Vedic, a DCS subset; vidyut-cheda's statistics come partly from DCS. The vidyut_first baseline is the first analysis of the oracle behind the vidyut route. Nothing here says how any of them does on text outside the DCS tradition.
- **Sandhi.** Every system was given the DCS's sandhi-resolved, compound-split words. Real Sanskrit text is sandhied; segmentation, the hardest step, is not measured. A system that must segment first would score lower.
- **What the key collapses.** Vedic accent, anusvāra vs class nasal, homonym digits (MW's *ā-pi* verb and *āpi* noun share a key; the sheet showed this), and vowel-sandhi at compound joints. It does not fold the `-ay` stems (*cint* vs *cintay*), the root vs participle (*gam* vs *gata*), or MW's feminine entries: each counts against a system that prints the other convention, exactly as the twins count convention differences.
- **The token unit.** A compound headword is a span of DCS words; a system's member lemmas must join back to MW's compound. vidyut-cheda and the vidyut baseline lemmatize compound members to roots, which is why their compound column is low.
- **Edition drift.** MW cites 19th-century editions (Calcutta Mahābhārata, Bombay or Gorresio Rāmāyaṇa, Brockhaus' Kathāsaritsāgara); v0 excludes the epics and Harivaṃśa because no machine concordance to the critical editions exists, and the verse alignment to GRETIL is a step the Greek and Latin twins did not need. The blind sheet checks the headword, not the passage.
- **Scope.** Only texts both in the DCS dump and verse-alignable: the Ṛgveda and Manu are 63% of the gold. Raghuvaṃśa, Śiśupālavadha, Rājataraṅgiṇī and the Bṛhatsaṃhitā are cited 2,400 times by MW and are not in the DCS dump.
- **Readers.** No human reader yet. The blind readers are models; the sheets are ready for a human.
- **A known relocation gap.** The compound join in `relocate_mw.py` compares member keys joined after the nasal fold, so compounds whose first member ends in a nasal before a stop (*sam-*, *kim-*) are under-located (140 not-located headwords begin with *saṃ*); fixing it changes the gold the blind sheet was drawn from, so it is left for v0.1 and stated here.
- **MW's list of works** (pp. xxxiii–xxxvi) is not available in machine form; the edition column of `mw_abbrev_map.json` is inferred where marked.
