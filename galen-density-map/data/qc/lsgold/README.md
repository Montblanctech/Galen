# LS-Gold — the Latin twin of LSJ-Gold (v0, 2026-11-08)

> **The current release is LS-Gold 1.0** (157,855 loci over 33 authors; `ls_gold_v1_0.csv`), the stable release: every doubtful locus read blind and decided, the rest sampled. See [`release/ls-gold-1.0/README.md`](../../../../release/ls-gold-1.0/README.md) and the reading record in [`grind/`](grind/). The text below is the v0 build log; its numbers are as of v0.

Lemmatizer accuracy on the **dictionary-cited stratum of Latin**: the tokens that Lewis & Short (1879) cites for a headword, relocated into the Perseus canonical-latinLit editions and validated by two independent offline morphological analysers. Same question as LSJ-Gold (`../lsjgold/`), same protocol, same honesty rules (report, never patch; every figure traces to a row; negative results ship). Built in one day from the Greek pipeline; the feasibility study that preceded it is `../../latin_feasibility/REPORT.md`.

## Sources and licences
- **Lewis & Short**: `PerseusDL/lexica`, `lat.ls.perseus-eng2.xml` (51,645 entries; 30,018 entries carry at least one Latin CTS citation; 280,550 work-level Latin citations, 253,107 of them into works held here). CC BY-SA 4.0, Perseus Digital Library. The gold derived from it is CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Texts**: `PerseusDL/canonical-latinLit`, 173 Latin TEI editions of 162 works (`data/latin_corpus/editions_manifest.json`; every file L&S cites that the repository holds, plus Horace's Odes, which L&S numbers phi0893.phi002 while the repository has them as phi001). CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Oracles**: Collatinus (pycollatinus 0.1.6, a Gaffiot/L&S-derived lexicon, all readings per form) and Whitaker's Words (whitakers_words 0.7.0, frequency class X = every stem). Both offline, both context-free, both return every lemma a form could belong to.

## How a locus is made (`pipeline/lsgold/`)
1. `fetch_editions.py` downloads the editions; `tokenize_editions.py` indexes each by CTS locator (textpart divs joined by `:`, `<l n>` lines for verse, section milestones for Pliny keyed book:section with the chapter as alias; decade/volume wrappers dropped) and freezes **the project's Latin token stream** (`data/latin_corpus/tokens/<stem>.jsonl`, `{i, surface, unit}`): letters only, enclitics on the host word, notes/apparatus/heads/speakers dropped. Every scored system is run on this stream pre-tokenised, so the Latin benchmark has **no alignment step** (the Greek one needed one because odyCy's stream was frozen first).
2. `relocate_la.py` extracts (headword = the entry's first `<orth>`, macrons off; locator; the `<quote>` that precedes the `<bibl>` inside a `<cit>`), resolves the locator (the forms tried, in order: as cited with empty segments dropped; without leading labels such as `Orat`; book:section when the edition has no chapter level; a unique unit one level deeper, for Juvenal satire:line, Seneca letter:section, Catullus poem:line; Plautus' scene-relative line numbers mapped onto the edition's through-numbered lines; prefix; trailing letter; shortened from the right), takes the cited unit ± 1 sibling as the window, and validates each surface in it: **collatinus** if Collatinus's analyses contain the headword key, else **whitaker** if Whitaker's do, else **quoted** if the surface equals L&S's quoted form (sharing the headword's first two letters) and neither oracle analyses the surface at all. Exactly one validated token in the window is a gold locus; when a headword occurs in several units of the window the cited unit decides if it holds it exactly once (`center_resolved` in the stats); several → `ambiguous`; none → `not_located`.
3. `score_la.py` compares each system's lemma at the gold token index with the headword: **blind** = `latnorm.key` equality (case, diacritics, u/v, i/j, homograph digits neutral; the headline), **strict** = u/v and i/j as printed. Strata: author, validation route, how often L&S cites the entry (1 / 2–5 / 6+). Wilson 95% everywhere. `leaderboard_la.py` renders `docs/ls-gold/`.
4. `validation_sheet_la.py` / `../lsjgold/validation_score.py`: the blind relocation check, 60 random loci + 10 anchors + 10 plants, key private until scored.

Comparison keys: `pipeline/lsgold/latnorm.py`. Systems (`latin_caches.py`, caches under `data/latin_corpus/caches/<system>/`): Stanza 1.15 la-ittb / la-perseus / la-proiel / la-llct, LatinCy la_core_web_lg, CLTK 1.5 backoff lemmatizer, and two context-free **baselines** that are the oracles' own first analysis (Collatinus-first, Whitaker-first), printed in italics because they are partly circular with the validation routes.

## Results (v0, 2026-11-08)
**Relocation** (`relocation_stats.json`, 509 s): of 253,107 L&S citations into the 162 held works, **161,032 located** (63.6%): 158,106 by Collatinus, 2,446 by Whitaker, 480 by the quoted form; 8,152 of them decided by the cited-unit rule. Ambiguous 15,059; not located 70,169 (common headwords at drifted lines: *volo*, *bonus*, *contra*, *tum*, *cum* top the list; 60% of these citations carry a quote, so a quote-anchored wider window is the obvious v0.1 gain; Cicero 22,926 and Plautus 14,445 lead, the latter because L&S numbers lines within scenes); locator not in our edition 6,847 (2.7%). The loci cover **20,830 distinct headwords (21,368 L&S entries) over 33 authors**: Cicero 31,974, Livy 17,542, Pliny 15,946, Ovid 15,216, Virgil 11,214, Quintilian 10,119, Horace 7,310, Tacitus 7,225, Caesar 6,211, Lucretius 5,523, Plautus 4,657 … By citation frequency of the entry: cited once 4,687 loci, 2–5 times 15,073, 6+ 141,272. Against LSJ-Gold 1.0's 70,033 Greek loci, the Latin set is 2.3× the size from one afternoon, because the Latin oracles are offline and complete where Morpheus had to be filled online.

**Blind relocation check** (`validation/`, seed 20261108, 60 random loci + 10 anchors + 10 plants, key private until scored): two model readers (sonnet, opus), both passed the gate (anchors 10/10, plants 10/10) and both said **yes on 60/60 random loci** (Wilson 94.0–100); agreement κ undefined (no variation). One reader noted that *levior* "smoother" belongs to L&S's *lēvis* (levis2), not *levis* "light": the gold has it under levis2 (the relocation chose the right entry), but the comparison key drops homograph digits, so a system printing either *levis* is counted right; homographs are collapsed, which is lenient by construction and stated here. No human reader yet.

**Systems** (`ls_gold_scores.json` / `ls_gold_table.md` / `docs/ls-gold/`, every cache complete over the 173 editions, all non-baseline rows on the same 161,032 loci): **LatinCy la_core_web_lg 84.94%** [84.77–85.11], **CLTK 1.5 backoff 83.96%** [83.78–84.14], Stanza la-proiel 75.47%, Stanza la-perseus 65.46%, Stanza la-ittb 65.26%, Stanza la-llct 44.40% (blind match; strict, keeping u/v and i/j as L&S prints them, runs 20 points lower for every system because the editions print *u* where L&S prints *v*). The two baselines, the oracles' own first analysis, sit between the two groups: Collatinus-first 82.26% on 159,918 loci, Whitaker-first 81.38% on 147,490 (each misses the loci it did not analyse), and they are partly circular with the validation routes, so they bound the table rather than compete in it. **The rare stratum repeats the Greek result.** On headwords L&S cites once (4,687 loci) the neural systems fall away from their 6+ figure: LatinCy 86.30 → 70.51, Stanza la-proiel 77.65 → 55.45, la-ittb 67.20 → 46.49, la-perseus 67.94 → 42.10, la-llct 45.45 → 33.06; the lexicon-backed CLTK barely moves (84.54 → 79.80) and the lexicon baselines rise (Collatinus-first 81.78 → 86.44, Whitaker-first 81.05 → 82.07), exactly the Dilemma pattern on LSJ-Gold (1.0: 92.9% on cited-once headwords against about 60% for the treebank systems). By author (29 authors with at least 100 loci) LatinCy leads on 20 and CLTK on nine: Pliny (80.4 vs 78.1), Sallust, Plautus, Phaedrus, Vitruvius, Valerius Flaccus, Valerius Maximus, Gellius and Martial; both drop below 80 on Lucan (73.7 / 70.1), Gellius and Martial, LatinCy also on Pliny, Vitruvius and Plautus, where Collatinus-first stays at 81–83. The 480 quoted-form loci (tokens neither oracle analyses) are hard for everyone: LatinCy 47.3%, CLTK 31.7%, the Stanza models 22–39%.

## Known residual errors (added 2026-11-11)

480 of the 161,032 loci are validated only by the form Lewis & Short quotes (neither oracle analyses the surface), and 438 of
those by one word of a quoted phrase that shares the headword's first two letters. That is the weakness LSJ-Gold 0.1 had
(see `release/lsj-gold-0.1.1/README.md`), on a small scale: of 30 such loci read, one was wrong (*digna* under *divus*,
from the quotation 'digna diva venustissima Venere'). Of the order of 15 loci (0.01%) may carry a wrong gold, far below any
system's interval. The next version will keep a quoted locus only when Lewis & Short quotes that single word, as LSJ-Gold
0.1.1 does.

## Honesty
L&S cites what its lexicographers found worth citing: the rare, dictionary-worthy stratum, not overall accuracy; treebank test scores are not comparable. The Perseus L&S XML's CTS tags cover 74% of its citations (the rest are plain-text abbreviations, see the feasibility report). L&S was written against older editions, so locators drift and the ±1 window absorbs part of that; the blind sheet measures what it does not absorb. The oracles are lexicon-driven: a token neither knows and that L&S does not quote is never a locus, which biases the set toward forms the lexica cover. Both oracles are also scored as baselines, so their circularity is visible, not hidden. Headword spelling is the gold's: a system that prints *vulnus* for L&S's *volnus*, *adtollo* for *attollo*, *inquam* for *inquit* or *sui* for *se* is counted wrong in both columns, exactly as the Greek benchmark counts convention differences against a system; the oracle-disagreement list in the build notes (306 Aeneid surfaces where Collatinus and Whitaker share no lemma) is the seed of an orthography-neutral column, not yet built. No human reader has judged a sheet yet.
