# MW-Gold: feasibility of a Sanskrit twin of LSJ-Gold / LS-Gold (2026-11-09, written before any relocation)

Question: can Monier-Williams' (1899) text citations be relocated onto tokens of open Sanskrit editions, validated, and used to score Sanskrit lemmatizers on the dictionary-cited stratum, the way `data/qc/lsjgold/` does for Greek and `data/qc/lsgold/` for Latin? Every count below is computed by `pipeline/mwgold/mw_parse.py` (citation census) and `pipeline/mwgold/tokenize_dcs.py` (DCS token streams) from the files named here; nothing was relocated or scored when this note was written. Predictions are in §7 and are the pre-registration for steps 2–5.

## 1. The dictionary
- **Source**: Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries, `sanskrit-lexicon/csl-orig`, `v02/mw/mw.txt` (50,163,993 bytes; copy in `mw_src/`, gitignored). Licence of the repository: **CC BY-SA 4.0** (`mw_src/LICENSE`). The older TEI header shipped with the file (`mw_src/mwheader.xml`, 2014) says CC BY-NC-SA 3.0; the live csl-guides page ("Abbreviations & Citations", read 2026-11-09) states that the per-dictionary repositories carry CC BY-SA 4.0 and asks that reuse cite both the print work and "Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries". The gold derived from MW therefore carries **CC BY-SA 4.0** with that double citation; the NC clause of the 2014 header is noted as a conflict in the README and the Cologne project's current licence is taken as governing (the repository LICENSE is the legal instrument).
- **Format** (`mw_src/mw-meta2.txt`): 286,003 entries, each a meta line `<L>id<pc>page<k1>headword<k2>headword-with-hyphens<h>hom<e>level` plus a body. Headwords are **SLP1** (`kuliSa` = kuliśa); `k2` keeps the compound hyphenation (`karaRa—grAma`), the body marks members with `<srs/>`. Citations are `<ls>Abbr. locus</ls>` or, continued, `<ls n="Abbr.">locus</ls>`.
- **Citations**: 319,829 `<ls>` tags; **53,843 carry a numeric locus** (`mw_citation_census.json`), 784 text abbreviations. The rest are bare text names (`MBh.`, `L.` = lexicographers, `W.` = Wilson) and are useless as gold. By abbreviation, the ten largest with loci: Pāṇ. 8,672 · RV. 8,342 · MBh. 5,071 · Mn. 3,760 · AV. 2,363 · Dhātup. 1,844 · R. 1,505 · BhP. 1,480 · Hariv. 1,180 · Uṇ. 760.
- **Citation format**: text abbreviation + comma-separated locus in MW's own scheme (`RV. i, 32, 5` = maṇḍala, hymn, verse; `Mn. ii, 54`; `ŚBr. iii, 1, 1, 21` = kāṇḍa, adhyāya, brāhmaṇa, kaṇḍikā; `MBh. iii, 14189` = parvan + verse counted through the parvan in the **Calcutta** edition; `Kathās. lii, 211` = taraṅga counted through the work (Brockhaus); `Śak. 304, 8` = page, line of MW's own edition). No CTS URNs exist; the abbreviation-to-edition map is `mw_abbrev_map.json` (expansions from Cologne's `mwauth` tooltip file; MW's printed "List of Works and Authors", pp. xxxiii–xxxvi, is not available in machine form: Cologne's preface transcription and scan series stop at p. xxxii, so the edition column is inferred from the locus shapes and from Böhtlingk–Roth's conventions, which MW follows, and is marked so).
- **Quoted forms**: MW does not quote the inflected form beside a citation (LSJ and L&S do). The `quoted` validation route of the twins has no Sanskrit counterpart; the headword alone is the claim.
- **Compounds**: 41,428 of the first 60,000 entries are hyphenated compounds in `k2`; the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit splits compounds into member tokens (`mahā` + `ṛṣayaḥ`, `Case=Cpd` on the non-final member), so a compound headword is a **span** of DCS tokens, not one token (§4).

## 2. The texts we can hold
- **Digital Corpus of Sanskrit (DCS)**, Oliver Hellwig, `OliverHellwig/sanskrit`, `dcs/data/conllu/files/` (sparse clone at `data/sanskrit_corpus/dcs_repo/`, 23,262 files, 1,095 MB, gitignored). **CC BY 4.0** (`dcs/data/readme.md`). 271 texts; each chapter a CoNLL-U file whose lines are **sandhi-resolved words with a human-annotated lemma** (`Annotator=Ol`), UPOS, morphology and `Unsandhied=` form. The DCS word is the token of this benchmark. The dump has **no verse numbers**: a file is a chapter (`ManuS, 1`; `ṚV, 1, 1` = hymn; `ŚBM, 3, 1, 1` = brāhmaṇa) and sentences are in order with database ids only. A verse-level locus therefore needs a second source (next bullet). Token streams frozen by `tokenize_dcs.py`: 36 texts, **2,719,370 tokens** (`data/sanskrit_corpus/tokenize_summary.json`), of which the v0 scope (§3) is 1.22 M.
- **Verse-numbered open editions to align with** (DCS sentence ↔ verse): (A) **GRETIL** TEI/plaintext (`gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/plaintext/sa_*.txt`): one reference per verse in a uniform form (`// Manu_1.1`, `RV_1,001.01`, `bhp_01.01.001`, `sokss_1,1.1`, `hit_0.1`, `GG_1.1`, `BC_1.1`; AV as the older TITUS-derived `avs___u.htm` with `(AVŚ_1,14.2a)`); licence per file, Manusmṛti **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, GRETIL's own terms "for scholarly reference, no commercial use" (we store only token indices and short contexts, never the text). (B) **Hellwig's Vedic Prose Corpus** (`corpus/VPC/` in the same repository, CC BY 4.0): one sentence per line with the full citation (`3.1.1.1.1` = ŚBM kāṇḍa.adhyāya.brāhmaṇa.kaṇḍikā.sentence; sūtra texts adhyāya.kaṇḍikā.sūtra) and the **same sentence text as the DCS dump**, so the join is exact string equality within a chapter (checked on ŚBM 10.1.1: identical). (C) **ambuda-org/dcs** (CC BY 4.0): DCS exports with verse ids (`# id = Ku.1.1`, `Kira.18.47`, `Shata.1.1`, `M.1.1.1`, `R.1.1.1`) and SLP1 token/lemma lines for 14 texts; joined to the DCS stream by lemma sequence.
- **SARIT** (TEI, CC BY-SA/NC per header) holds śāstra and commentaries MW rarely cites with loci; not needed for v0.

## 4. The sandhi problem and the token unit
MW cites headwords; printed text is sandhied and compounds are unbroken. DCS resolves both by hand: the stream is pre-segmented, so **relocation needs no sandhi tool**, but the benchmark's token is the DCS word, and every scored system must be given the segmented words (pre-tokenised, as the Latin twin does) or have its own segmentation aligned back to them. Consequences, decided now: (i) single-token loci: the DCS lemma of the token equals the headword key; (ii) **compound loci**: a run of consecutive DCS tokens, all but the last marked `Case=Cpd`, whose lemmas joined with vowel sandhi (`sktnorm.join_members`) equal the headword, or whose member sequence equals MW's `k2` hyphenation; such a locus points at the span (`token_index` = first token, `span` = length) and a system is right when its lemmas for the span join to the headword; (iii) verbs: MW's headword is the root (`gam`, `abhi-gam`), DCS's lemma is the root with preverbs (`abhigam`) and class-10/causative stems in `-ay` (`pratipūjay`, `cintay`); the blind key does **not** fold `-ay` (that would equate *cint* with *cintay* by fiat); the failure catalogue will count how much of the gap is this convention.

## 5. Oracles
- **DCS lemma** (human gold): a token validates a citation when `key(DCS lemma) == key(MW headword)` (route `dcs`), or the compound rule (route `dcs_cpd`). This is the strongest oracle available in any of the three languages, and it is **circular with every system trained on DCS** (ByT5-Sanskrit's fine-tuning data is DCS; Stanza-sa is trained on UD_Sanskrit-Vedic, a 27k-word subset of DCS's Ṛgveda annotation). The README will say so in its first paragraph.
- **vidyut-kosha** (Ambuda, Rust/Python, 0.4.0, MIT; data 32 MB): a Pāṇinian word generator; `Kosha.get(pada)` returns every analysis of a (segmented) form with its lemma. Independent of DCS. Tested: works in SLP1, covers inflected nouns and finite verbs (manum → manu; abruvan → brū; devam 63 analyses), misses some sandhi-final forms (ṛṣayaḥ, rāmaḥ returned nothing as typed: input must be pausa form in SLP1, `fzayas` not `fzayaH`; to be handled). Route `vidyut`: used when the DCS lemma differs from the headword but vidyut's analyses of the unsandhied form contain the headword key and the DCS lemma shares its first three letters (a convention difference, not a different word); reported as its own column like the Latin `quoted` route.
- **Sanskrit Heritage** analyser (INRIA, OCaml, CeCILL-C): the pip wrapper ships Linux binaries only; the web API is an outward call. Not used in v0; named as the independent oracle for v0.1 on a Linux box.

## 7. Plan and predictions (pre-registered)
1. `align_verses.py`: for tier A, concatenate each DCS chapter's sentences and the corresponding GRETIL verses as **consonant skeletons** (vowels, visarga and anusvāra removed, so sandhi mostly vanishes), align with difflib, give each DCS sentence the verse whose span covers most of its characters; for tier B join by exact sentence text (VPC) or lemma sequence (ambuda). Report aligned-sentence rates per text. **Prediction**: ≥ 90% of sentences aligned for Manu, RV, BhP, Kum, Kir; lower for AV (TITUS orthography) and Kathās (edition differences).
2. `relocate_mw.py`: window = cited verse ± 1 (chapter for the B′ texts, flagged), validation `dcs` / `dcs_cpd` / `vidyut`, exactly one candidate → gold, several → ambiguous, none → not located, verse unknown → `locator_not_in_edition`. **Predictions**: located **45–65%** of the 19,076 in-scope citations (Latin 63.6%, Greek 38%): **8,500–12,400 loci**; Pāṇ. ≤ 10%; compound loci 25–40% of the gold; `vidyut` route under 10%; ambiguity low (a śloka is ~16 words) except RV, where MW cites common words in a short verse; not-located dominated by (a) verse-number drift between MW's editions and GRETIL's (Manu Kullūka vs Jolly, BhP Bombay vs Burnouf, Kathās Brockhaus vs Durgāprasād), (b) headword/lemma convention (`-ay` stems, u/ū stems, MW's `-in`/`-ī` feminines), (c) DCS chapters missing (BhP, Kathās are partial). The 5,000 threshold is reached by RV + Mn alone at a 45% rate.
3. Blind check as in the twins (60 random + 10 anchors + 10 plants; the reader sees the DCS sentence with the token bracketed and the MW headword in IAST; anchors = loci whose surface equals the headword's citation form; plants = another headword of the same first letter); two model readers; gate anchors ≥ 9/10, plants ≥ 8/10. **Prediction**: random-locus precision ≥ 93% (DCS lemma is human gold; the residual error is the compound join and the verse alignment landing on a neighbour verse that happens to hold the same word).
4. Scoring: blind = `sktnorm.key` (nasal fold, accent off), strict = as printed; strata by text, validation route, compound vs single, MW citation frequency (1 / 2–5 / 6+). **Predictions**: ByT5-Sanskrit ≥ 95% blind (it has seen these sentences), Stanza-sa 70–85% overall and ≥ 90% on RV (training overlap), vidyut-cheda 60–80%; on compound spans every system lower; on cited-once headwords the usual drop of 10–25 points for the trained systems.
5. CPU time: ByT5 on ~10k gold sentences at an estimated 2–5 s each on CPU = 6–14 h detached; if that does not finish, the ByT5 row is reported on the sentences done, with n.

## 8. Risks named before starting
- The circularity is structural: the only human-lemmatized Sanskrit corpus is also the training data of the best systems. MW-Gold measures **agreement with DCS on MW-cited tokens**; for DCS-trained systems that is a consistency check, not a generalisation score. The independent part of the benchmark is the vidyut route and the systems not trained on DCS.
- Verse alignment is a new step the Latin twin did not need; its error enters the gold. The blind check measures the headword, not the passage; a locus may be a true instance of the headword in the wrong verse. A per-text alignment audit (20 sentences each, read against GRETIL) is added to step 2.
- GRETIL's NC licence: the gold stores token indices into the DCS stream (CC BY), the DCS surface and a ±8-word DCS context; nothing from GRETIL is redistributed.
- MW's edition identifications are inferred where the printed list could not be read; `mw_abbrev_map.json` marks each.
