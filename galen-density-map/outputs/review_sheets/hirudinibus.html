Περὶ ἐγχαράξεως καὶ κατασχασμοῦ. Ἐγχαράξομεν μέρη τοῦ σώματος, ἤτοι φλεγμαίνοντα, ἢ σκιῤῥούμενα, ἢ τεταμένα, ἢ ἐπωδύνως ἔχοντα, ἢ ῥευματισθέντα, ἤδη στάσιν εἰληφότος τοῦ ῥευματισμοῦ, ἢ δριμείας ὕλης παρακειμένης, ἢ ἰώδους δυνάμεως ἔξωθεν ἐμπεσούσης, ἢ βουλόμενοι μεταγαγεῖν ἐξ ἑτέρων μερῶν εἰς ἄλλο τὴν ὕλην. οἷον σκέλη κατασχάζομεν κεφαλῆς πεπονθυίας ἢ πλεοναζούσης ὕλης ἐν τῷ σώματι μειῶσαι θέλοντες. καὶ μάλισθ’ ὅταν ὁ πλεονασμὸς γένηται δι’ ἐποχὴν ὕλης εἰωθυίας ἀποκρίνεσθαι συνήθως.

RENDERING 1

At the [321] beginning, however, one must use repellents. The cupping-glass is able to evacuate the matter, to resolve pain, to diminish inflammation, to disperse flatulence, to restore the appetites, to make a slack stomach toned, to deliver from faintings, to transfer the fluxes from the deep parts and to dry them, and to check haemorrhages, and to draw off the corruptive powers of the menses, and to relieve the menses. On incision and scarification. We incise parts of the body which are either inflamed, or becoming scirrhous, or tense, or in a painful condition, or affected by flux, when the flux has already come to a standstill, or when acrid matter lies adjacent, or when a venomous power has fallen upon them from without, or when we wish to transfer the matter from certain parts to another. For example, we scarify the legs when the head is affected, or when we wish to diminish matter that is in excess in the body, and especially whenever the excess has arisen through the retention of matter which is habitually accustomed to be excreted.

RENDERING 2

At the [321] beginning, however, one must use repellent remedies. The cupping-glass is able to evacuate the matter, relieve pain, diminish inflammation, disperse an inflation with pneuma, restore the appetites, make an atonic stomach toned, deliver from faintings, transfer fluxes out of the depths and dry them, check haemorrhages, draw off the destructive powers of the menses, and lighten the menses. On Incision and Scarification. We shall incise parts of the body that are inflamed, or becoming scirrhous, or distended, or painful, or that have received a flux, when the flux has already come to a standstill; or when acrid matter lies alongside; or when a venomous power has fallen upon them from outside; or when we wish to transfer the matter from some parts to another. For instance, we scarify the legs when the head is affected, or when we wish to diminish matter that is in excess in the body, and especially when the excess has arisen through the retention of matter that is habitually accustomed to be excreted.

ἀντισπᾷ δὲ καὶ τὰ δριμέα φάρμακα τοῖς κόλποις ἐπιτιθέμενα [320] τὰς ἐπὶ κεφαλὴν καὶ σπλάγχνα ῥοπὰς τῶν χυμῶν. καὶ ἁπλῶς τὴν ἀντίσπασιν ποιητέον ἐπὶ μὲν τοῖς ἄνω ῥέουσι χυμοῖς εἰς τὰ κάτω, ἐπὶ δὲ τοῖς εἰς τὰ κάτω τοὐναντίον, καὶ ἐπὶ μὲν ταῖς ἔσω ῥοπαῖς ἔξω, καὶ πάλιν ἐπὶ ταῖς ἐκτὸς εἴσω, κᾂν πρὸς τὰ δεξιὰ ῥέπῃ, ἐπὶ τὰ ἀριστερὰ, καὶ εἰ πρὸς τὰ εὐώνυμα, πρὸς τὰ ἐναντία. οὕτω δὲ καὶ τὰς μὲν ὀπίσω ῥοπὰς πρόσω, τὰς δ’ εἰς πρόσω ὄπισθεν. Περὶ σικύας. Σικύαι προκενωθέντων χρήσιμοι. πληθωρικῶν γὰρ ὑπαρχόντων οὐ χρησόμεθα ταύταις. τῷ δὲ αὐτῷ λόγῳ κἀπὶ τῶν κατ’ ἐγκέφαλον καὶ μήνιγγας φλεγμονῶν οὐ χρησόμεθα σικύαις ἐν ἀρχῇ τῶν παθῶν, ὥσπερ οὐδ’ ἐπ’ ἄλλου μορίου φλεγμαίνοντος οὐδενὸς, ἀλλ’ ὅταν μὴ ἐπιῤῥῇ μηδὲν ἔτι καὶ προκενώσωμεν ὅλον τὸ σῶμα, χρεία τε γένηται ἢ κινῆσαί τε καὶ ἐκμοχλεῦσαι τὸ καταφλεγμαῖνον, ἢ πρὸς τοὐκτὸς ἐπισπάσασθαι. γενομένων δὲ ἔτι τῶν παθῶν, οὐκ αὐτοῖς τοῖς ἀρχομένοις κάμνειν μέλεσιν, ἀλλὰ τοῖς συνεχέσιν αὐτῶν ἐπιθετέον τὴν σικύαν ἀντισπάσεως ἕνεκεν.

RENDERING 1

Acrid drugs, too, applied to the kolpoi [320] draw back by revulsion the inclinations of the humours toward the head and the viscera. And, in general, revulsion is to be performed, in the case of humours flowing upward, toward the parts below, and in the case of those flowing toward the parts below, the opposite; in the case of inward inclinations, outward, and again in the case of outward ones, inward; and if the flow inclines toward the right, toward the left, and if toward the left-hand side, toward the opposite parts. So too, the inclinations backward are to be drawn forward, and those toward the front, backward. On the cupping-glass. Cupping-glasses are useful after prior evacuation; for when patients are plethoric we shall not use them. By the same reasoning, in inflammations of the brain and the meninges too we shall not use cupping-glasses at the beginning of the affections, just as not in the case of any other inflamed part either, but when nothing is any longer flowing in and we have first evacuated the whole body, and there arises a need either to set in motion and lever out what is inflamed, or to draw it toward the outside. But while the affections are still coming on, the cupping-glass is to be applied not to the very limbs that are beginning to suffer, but to the parts continuous with them, for the sake of revulsion.

RENDERING 2

Acrid drugs applied to the hollows (κόλποις) also draw away by revulsion [320] the inclinations of the humours toward the head and the viscera. And in general, revulsion is to be made, for humours flowing upward, toward the lower parts, and for those flowing toward the lower parts, the opposite; and for inward inclinations, outward, and again for outward ones, inward; and if the inclination is toward the right, toward the left, and if toward the left, toward the opposite side. So too backward inclinations are to be drawn forward, and forward ones backward. On the cupping-glass. Cupping-glasses are useful when the body has first been evacuated; for when patients are plethoric we shall not use them. By the same reasoning, in inflammations of the brain and the meninges too we shall not use cupping-glasses at the beginning of the affections, just as we shall not with any other inflamed part either, but only when nothing is any longer flowing in and we have first evacuated the whole body, and there arises a need either to set in motion and lever out what is inflamed, or to draw it toward the outside. While the affections are still coming into being, the cupping-glass is to be applied not to the very limbs that are beginning to suffer, but to the parts continuous with them, for the sake of revulsion.

οἷον αἱμοῤῥοΐδων ἐπισχεθεισῶν ἐγχαράσσομεν σκέλη προλουσάμενοι ἢ ἐν θερμῷ πυριᾷν ἢ [322] σπόγγῳ. τὸ μὲν γὰρ φλέβα διελεῖν πολλάκις τοῦ ἔτους οὐκ ἐπιτήδειον ἐνόμισα. ἅμα γὰρ αἵματι πολλῷ συνεκκρίνεται τὸ ζωτικὸν πνεῦμα. τούτου δὲ ἀναλισκομένου πυκνότερον ὅ τε ὅλος ὄγκος καταψύχεται καὶ πάντα τὰ ψυχικὰ ἔργα χεῖρον γίνεται. διὰ τοῦτο ἀπὸ τῶν ἀκυρωτέρων, οἷον σκελῶν, τὴν ἀφαίρεσιν δεῖ ποιεῖσθαι. ὠφελεῖ δὲ ἡ ἐγχάραξις καὶ ὀφθαλμοὺς χρονίως ῥευματιζομένους καὶ κεφαλῆς διαθέσεις καὶ τὰ περὶ θώρακα καὶ μετάφρενα καὶ συνάγχας καὶ σφηνώσεις.

RENDERING 1

For example, when haemorrhoids have been suppressed, we incise the legs, having first bathed [the patient], or fomenting either with hot [water] or [322] with a sponge. For I have not considered it suitable to divide a vein many times in the year, since the vital pneuma is excreted together with a large quantity of blood. And when this is consumed rather frequently, the whole bulk [of the body] is chilled and all the psychic functions become worse. For this reason the withdrawal must be made from the less important [parts], such as the legs. Incision also benefits eyes chronically affected by flux, and affections of the head, and conditions about the chest and the upper back, and synanchai and impactions (sphēnōseis).

RENDERING 2

For example, when haemorrhoids have been suppressed, we scarify the legs, having first bathed the patient, or fomenting (πυριᾷν) either in hot water or [322] with a sponge. For I have not considered it suitable to open a vein many times in the year; for together with a great quantity of blood the vital pneuma is evacuated along with it, and when this is consumed too frequently, the whole bodily mass is chilled and all the psychic functions become worse. For this reason the removal should be made from the less important parts, such as the legs. Scarification also benefits eyes chronically affected by flux, conditions of the head, the affections about the chest and the back between the shoulders, and quinsies and impactions.

Περὶ ἀντισπάσεως. Τὰς σφοδροτάτας ἐπιῤῥοὰς τῶν χυμῶν ἀντισπαστικοῖς βοηθήμασι κωλύομεν ἀθρόως κατασκήπτειν. ἀντισπαστικὰ δέ ἐστι βοηθήματα ἐπὶ τῆς εἰς θώρακα καὶ γαστέρα ῥοπῆς εἰς χεῖρας· ἔμετοι δὲ ἐπὶ τῆς εἰς τὰ κάτω, καθάπερ καὶ ἡ διὰ τῶν δριμυτέρων κλυσμῶν τῆς ἐπὶ τοὺς ἐμέτους. ἄμφω δὲ ταύτας τὰς ῥοπὰς, λέγω δὲ τὰς διὰ τῆς ἄνω καὶ κάτω γαστρὸς, εἰς οὖρα καὶ ἱδρῶτας ἀντισπάσεις· καὶ μέντοι γε καὶ τὰ οὖρα πρὸς ἱδρῶτάς τε καὶ διαχωρήσεις γαστρὸς. ἀντισπαστικὸν δὲ βοήθημα καὶ ἡ παρὰ τοὺς τιτθοὺς προβαλλομένη σικύα. ταῖς δὲ καθ’ ὑποχόνδριον ἐρειδομέναις ἀντισπάσεις τὴν ἐπὶ τοὺς μυκτῆρας ὁρμὴν τοῦ αἵματος, ὥσπερ γε καὶ τὴν διὰ μήτρας ἄμετρον φοράν.

RENDERING 1

We use them in place of cupping-glasses, and we pull them off whenever we judge that [319] half the quantity of blood has been drawn. We check it and do not allow it to flow out until a sufficient amount has been separated off, since the part is cooled both by the leeches, which are cold by nature, and by the surrounding air. On revulsion. The most violent influxes of the humours we prevent, by means of revulsive remedies, from falling upon a part all at once. Revulsive remedies, in the case of an inclination towards the thorax and the belly, are those directed towards the hands; vomitings in the case of an inclination towards the lower parts, just as the revulsion by means of the sharper clysters is revulsive of that towards vomiting. Both of these inclinations, I mean those through the upper and the lower belly, you will divert by revulsion towards urines and sweats; and indeed the urines too towards sweats and evacuations of the belly. A revulsive remedy too is the cupping-glass applied beside the breasts. And with those pressed firmly upon the hypochondrium you will revulse the rush of blood towards the nostrils, just as also the immoderate flow through the womb.

RENDERING 2

We use them in place of cupping-glasses, and we pull them off whenever we judge that half the [319] portion of the blood has been drawn. And we also hinder it and do not allow it to flow off until a sufficient amount has been discharged, since the part is chilled both by the leeches, which are cold by nature, and by the ambient air. On revulsion. The most violent influxes of the humours we prevent, by revulsive remedies, from settling down all at once. Revulsive remedies, in the case of an inclination towards the thorax and the belly, are [those that draw] to the hands; vomitings, in the case of one towards the lower parts, just as also the [revulsion] by means of the more acrid clysters [is revulsive] of the [inclination] towards vomitings. Both these inclinations, I mean those through the upper and the lower belly, you will draw back towards urine and sweats; and indeed you will also draw back urine towards sweats and evacuations of the bowel. The cupping-glass applied beside the breasts is also a revulsive remedy. With those fixed upon the hypochondrium you will draw back the rush of the blood towards the nostrils, as also the immoderate flow through the uterus.

RENDERING 1

One must [318] put them into lukewarm water, in a clean, wide vessel; then, taking them up with a sponge and cleaning off the slimy matter, we shall apply them with our hands. After they have fastened on, we pour warm oil over the part, so that it is not chilled. In the case of hands or feet, one must put the part itself into the water where the leeches are. If they should take hold only a little, one must snip off their tails; for as the blood pours out, they do not cease drawing until we sprinkle salt or ash upon their mouths. After they have fallen off, one must draw out the venomous matter with a cupping-glass; if not, one must foment with sponges. As for the bodies (of the bitten places), if there is slight oozing, cumin or flour is to be sprinkled on, and then it is to be bound up with wool together with a little oil (ἐρίῳ ἔλαιον βραχὺ κατειλικτέον). But if there should be a haemorrhage, linen cloths soaked in vinegar are to be applied, or burnt oak-gall, or one must soak a sponge in liquid pitch, burn it, and apply it. And one must know that leeches do not draw the blood that lies in the depth, but suck in that which lies next to the flesh.

RENDERING 2

One must [318] put them into lukewarm water and a clean, wide vessel; then, taking them up in a sponge and cleaning off the slimy matter, we shall apply them by hand. After they have fastened on, we pour lukewarm oil over the part so that it does not grow cold. In the case of the hands or feet, the part itself should be put into the water in which the leeches are. If they take hold only slightly, one should snip off their tails with scissors; for as the blood flows out they do not stop drawing until we sprinkle salt or ash on their mouths. After they have fallen off, the venomous matter should be drawn out with a cupping-glass; failing that, one should foment with sponges. As for the bodies (σώματα), if they ooze a little, cumin or meal is to be sprinkled on, and then a little oil is to be bound on with wool. If there is a haemorrhage, linen cloths soaked in vinegar are to be laid on, or burnt oak-gall; or one should soak a sponge in liquid pitch, burn it and apply it. One must know that leeches do not draw the blood that is in the depth, but suck in only the blood that lies next to the flesh.

RENDERING 1

[317] GALEN, ON LEECHES, REVULSION, THE CUPPING-GLASS, AND INCISION AND SCARIFICATION. Some people, having caught leeches, shut them up and use them on many occasions. For these, having been made gentle, readily attach themselves to the flesh. Those newly caught, however, should be kept for one day, with a little blood put in for their sustenance; for in this way their venomous matter will be squeezed out. At the time of use, let the part that is to be leeched first be cleansed with nitre and smeared, or scratched with the nails; for they will attach more readily.

RENDERING 2

[317] GALEN ON LEECHES, REVULSION, THE CUPPING-GLASS, AND INCISION AND SCARIFICATION. Some who hunt leeches shut them up and use them for many conditions. For these, once tamed, readily take hold of the flesh. Those newly caught must be kept for one day, with a little blood thrown in to them for nourishment; for in this way their venomous quality will be squeezed out. At the time of use, let the part that is to be leeched be first rubbed with natron and smeared over, or scratched with the nails, for so they will take hold more readily.
