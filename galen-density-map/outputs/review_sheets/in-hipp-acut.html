RENDERING 1

[580] For the purpose of properly establishing the regimen in those who are acutely ill, as he has taught, he bids us add in the examination of the strength and the character of the disease, and further the nature of the human being and his habit. For the chief point, which he establishes, is not to change the form of the regimen before the signs of concoction have become clear; and this will be possible once you have foreknown in how many days the disease will arrive at its acme. But since the regimen, which I shall examine (ἐξελέγξω), is capable of being varied in respect of the more and the less, because the patients too differ from one another in the strength and weakness of the faculty and in natural temperament and in the manner of the disease and in the pre-existing habit, by looking to these things it will be possible for you to aim, in accordance with the art, at the more and the less of the form of the regimen: whether one ought to regulate the regimen of the patient on oxymel or melikraton, or to give water alone to drink until the signs of concoction, or whether ptisan is to be given or only its juice, and whether twice or indeed once a day.

RENDERING 2

[580] For the proper establishment of the regimen in those who are acutely ill, as he has taught, he bids us add both the consideration of the strength and the manner of the disease, and further the nature of the person and his habit. For the main point, which he establishes, is not to change the form of the regimen before the signs of coction have become clear; and this will be the case once you have foreknown in how many days the disease will arrive at its peak. But since the regimen, which I shall scrutinise, can be varied according to the more and the less, because the patients too differ from one another in the robustness and feebleness of the capacity, in the natural mixture, in the manner of the disease and in the previous habit, it will be possible for you, looking to these things, to conjecture skilfully the more and less of the form of the regimen: whether one ought to regulate the diet of the patient on oxymel or on melicrat, or to give water alone to drink until the recognisable signs of coction; or whether ptisan is to be given, or only its juice; and whether twice, or even once, a day.

In this way, then, pain of the side also arises in the pleuritic affections, which were the subject of his discussion: the inflammation in these regions both distresses the parts through dyscrasia, by the abundance of the unnatural heat, and stretches the inflamed bodies by its repletion.
