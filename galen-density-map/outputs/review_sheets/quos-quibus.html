RENDERING 1

[343] GALEN, WHOM ONE MUST PURGE, AND WITH WHAT CATHARTICS, AND WHEN. Those who have healthy bodies are troublesome to purge; for they become dizzy and suffer griping, and the purgation proceeds with difficulty in them, and in addition to this they are, moreover, quickly exhausted. All these things come about because the cathartic drug strives to draw the humour proper to it, and, being at a loss for it, melts down the blood and the flesh, in order that it may draw from these what is proper to it.

RENDERING 2

[343] GALEN: WHOM ONE MUST PURGE, WITH WHAT CATHARTICS, AND WHEN. It is a laborious matter to purge those who have their bodies in a healthy state; for they become dizzy and suffer gripings, the purgation proceeds with difficulty in them, and besides this they also quickly become faint. All these things happen because the cathartic drug strives to draw its proper humour, and, through lacking it, colliquates the blood and the flesh, so that it may draw from them what is proper to it.

RENDERING 1

[354] Since it is prepared in various ways, the one that has taken in hellebore but does not contain scammony is a most excellent drug for purging downwards. After the purgative has been taken, it is beneficial to sip some ptisan after it, as Hippocrates says. For the cathartic drug itself, being small in quantity, reaches the bottom of the belly. But along its passage the gullet (stomachos), and as much of the stomach as lies high up, take up not only the quality of the purging agent but also its substance, which settles in the passage, and they are greatly harmed. The juice of the ptisan, therefore, is useful when sipped afterwards. It can wash off what has stuck in the passage and sweep it downward, and it can temper and alter the quality of the drug that has settled in the parts. For these reasons, then, he bids one sip ptisan after the drinking of the cathartic drug. But once the purgation has begun to take place, he no longer wishes ptisan to be given, taking care lest the action of the purging drug be weakened.

RENDERING 2

[354] And since it is prepared in various ways, the version that has taken hellebore but does not contain scammony is the best laxative drug. After the cathartic has been taken it is beneficial to sip ptisane afterwards, as Hippocrates says. For the cathartic drug itself, being small in quantity, arrives at the bottom of the belly, but along its passage the gullet and whatever is high up around the stomach, partaking not only of the quality of the purging agent but also of its substance adhering in the passage, is greatly harmed. The juice of ptisane sipped afterwards is therefore useful, as being able to wash off and sweep downwards what has been plastered on in the passage, and to temper and alter the quality of the drug that has settled into the parts. For these reasons, then, upon the drinking of the cathartic drug he bids one sip ptisane afterwards.
