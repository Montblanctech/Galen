RENDERING 1

And again another thing still greater than this: on the olecranon, the shin and the knee, where there is not even flesh, but only the periosteal membrane or a broad tendon lies beneath. So for abrasions [418] you would have this drug as the best, but for sinuses and abscesses it is neither the best of all nor second in excellence to the best. In these affections when they are of moderate size, however, in those with soft skin, and in women, children and old men, it is a good drug. So here too Heras has borne more witness to the drug than he ought. And he did so much more when he praised it for bites, saying that it suits all of them. For what does the 'all' added to the statement mean? Does he mean even if the creature that bit is a wild beast, or does he speak only of the bites of all human beings? Yet each is false. In the case of wild beasts it is plainly so: no one would say that wounds which are cured with difficulty by drastic drugs can be successfully treated by this one. And if he is speaking about human beings, even so the statement is so lacking in distinctions as to harm rather than benefit those who hear it. Someone, at any rate, persuaded one of the physicians to apply this plaster to a young man bitten on the index finger in the pankration. He fared so badly that the whole bitten part was in danger of rotting away, since putrefaction had supervened in it.

RENDERING 2

At any rate someone persuaded one of the physicians to apply this plaster to a young man who had been bitten on the index finger in the pankration, who was in such a bad state that the whole bitten part was in danger of rotting away, putrefaction having supervened upon it.

Μύρον δὲ τοῦτο τῶν μετρίως ἐστὶν καλῶν, [1055] Ἱκανῶς δὲ φαρμακῶδες, ὡς οὐκ ἄλλο τι. Ἢ ταὐτὸν ὁμοίως ἰρίνου Σεργητικοῦ, Ἢ Κυζικηνοῦ, τοῦτο δὲ ἀμαρακίνου. Τούτοις δὲ χρήσει μὴ παρόντος, ὡς ἔφην, Τοῦ μαλαβάθρου. μέτριον γὰρ οἶμαι μυράκοπον, Οὐδὲν ἔλαττον ὠφελοῦν τοὺς χρωμένους. Σκεύαζε δ’ οὕτως. ἀναλεάνας ἐπιμελῶς Πρόσβαλλε τὸ μέλι καὶ λεάνας συμμέτρως, Στακτήν τε μίξας κὠποβάλσαμον, πάλιν Λέαινε ἐπιμελῶς καθαρὰν τερμινθίνην, Κηρόν τε καὶ τὸ κρόκινον εἰς διπλοῦν βαλὼν Ἀγγεῖον ὕδατι τῆκε τὰ προειρημένα, Περὶ δεῖλον ἐν ἑτέρᾳ χύτρᾳ.

RENDERING 1

This unguent is among the moderately good ones, [1055] but it is sufficiently drug-like, as no other is. Or likewise the same amount of Sergetic iris unguent, or of Cyzicene, that is, of marjoram unguent. You will use these, as I said, when the malabathrum is not at hand. For I consider it a moderate myracopon, no less beneficial to those who use it. Prepare it thus: having first ground [the ingredients] up carefully, add the honey, and having ground it fine in due measure and mixed in the stacte and the opobalsam, grind carefully again. Then put pure terebinth resin, wax and the saffron unguent into a double vessel and melt the aforementioned with water, about evening (?) [περὶ δεῖλον], in another pot.

RENDERING 2

This perfume is among those that are moderately good, [1055] but sufficiently medicinal, as no other is. Or the same amount likewise of Sergetic iris-unguent, or of Cyzicene, and this of amaracine (marjoram unguent). You will use these when the malabathrum is not at hand, as I said; for I think it a moderate myrakopon (perfumed akopon), benefiting those who use it no less. Prepare it thus: having ground up carefully, add the honey, and having ground fine in due measure, and having mixed in the stacte and the opobalsam, again grind carefully the clean terebinth; and having put the wax and the crocine into a double vessel, melt the aforesaid in water, towards evening, in another pot.
