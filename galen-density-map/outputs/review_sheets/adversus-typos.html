Ἐπεὶ δ’ ἠνάγκασαν ἡμᾶς ἐάσαντας χρήσιμα τὸν χρόνον εἰς τὴν πρὸς αὐτοὺς ἀντιλογίαν ἀναλίσκειν, ἐπιδείξωμεν αὐτοῖς, εἰς ὅσον ἀναισθησίας ἥκουσιν αὐτοί τε καὶ οἱ προσέχοντες αὐτοῖς, ὥς τι διδάσκουσι χρήσιμον.

RENDERING 1

But for me it is enough merely to have pointed out the way of this waste [499] of time over such matters. And indeed the period of eighteen hours too (for once I have pointed out its way as well, I shall pass on to something else) is that of four quartans, of eight seventh-day fevers, and of twelve tenth-day fevers. And for all of these there is a method for each one, so that one can reckon it on the fingers without needing a diagram. But I, by way of a pastime, answer each of these on the spot to those who put them to me first, because I was trained in numbers from childhood. An untrained man, however, needs not months but whole years for them, in order to become wise in useless matters, which some people are not ashamed to propose to one another in earnest. That all those who wrote treatises on types err in two ways, neither teaching the forms of the diseases nor spending their time on useful things, I think has been sufficiently shown. But since they have compelled us to leave useful things aside and spend our time on the refutation of them, let us show them to what a degree of obtuseness they have come, both they themselves and those who attend to them as though they taught something useful.

RENDERING 2

But for me it suffices merely to have indicated the road of the waste [499] of time involved in such matters. For indeed the period of eighteen hours as well—for after indicating the road of this one too I shall pass on to something else—is one of four quartans, of eight septans, and of twelve decans. And for each of all these there is a method, so that one may reckon them on one's fingers without needing a diagram. But I for my part, in virtue of my education, answer each of these on the spot to those who put them to me, because I was exercised in numbers in my boyhood years; whereas an unexercised man needs not months but whole years for them, in order to become wise in useless matters, which some are not ashamed to propose to one another with earnest zeal. That, then, all who have written treatises on types err in a twofold way, neither teaching the forms of the diseases nor spending their time on useful things, I consider to have been sufficiently shown. But since they have compelled us to leave aside useful things and expend our time on the refutation directed against them, let us demonstrate to them to what degree of insensibility they have come, both they themselves and those who attend to them as though they were teaching something useful.

RENDERING 1

But we [487] know how greatly a single quotidian differs from two tertians in the treatment it requires, and tertians from quartans, so that it is not possible to distinguish them in the manner of those who are ignorant of the form (ἰδέα) of each fever. And often, in the case of patients, we have seen those treating them take an interweaving of two tertians to be a single quotidian. We had difficulty persuading them that the patient was afflicted by two tertians, until the earlier of the two had resolved first and only the more severe one remained.

RENDERING 2

But we [487] know how greatly different a treatment is required by a single quotidian as compared with two tertians, and by tertians as compared with two quartans, inasmuch as it is not possible to distinguish them in the manner of those who do not know the form of each fever. And often in the case of the sick we have seen the attending physicians supposing a complication of two tertians to be a single quotidian, and we persuaded them only with difficulty that the patient was being troubled by two tertians, when the earlier of them had been resolved first and only the more severe one was left behind.

ἐν τούτῳ τῷ χρόνῳ πρῶτον ἀκούσας ἐθαύμασα τοὺς προβάλλοντας ὡδί πως· ἐὰν πέντε τις ὥραις παροξυνόμενος ἀνίηται δυοῖν, ὡς εἶναι τὴν ὅλην [479] περίοδον ὡρῶν ζ΄ καὶ τοῦτ’ ἐφεξῆς γίγνηται, τίνα χρὴ νομίζειν τὸν τύπον; ἀπεκρινάμην οὖν εὐθέως αὐτῷ, μηδ’ οἷόν τε γενέσθαι τοῦτο. τοὺς γὰρ ὀλιγοχρονίους οὕτω παροξυσμοὺς μακρὰς ποιεῖσθαι τὰς παρακμὰς, καὶ τοὐπίπαν γε καὶ εἰς ἀπυρεξίαν ἀφικνεῖσθαι. σὺ δὲ παροξυσμὸν μὲν, ἔφην, διηγῇ νοσήματος ἐπιεικέστατον, τῇ συνεχείᾳ δὲ τῶν περιόδων ὀλεθριώτατον αὖθις ἀποφαίνεις αὐτόν. μηδὲν, ἔφη, σκόπει τό γε νῦν, πότερον ἀδύνατός ἐστιν ἡ ὑπόθεσις, ἢ δυνατὴ, γυμνασίας δ’ ἕνεκα τὰ τοιαῦτα συγχώρει ζητεῖσθαι. πρὸς γὰρ τὴν τῶν ποικίλων τύπων εὕρεσιν εὐπορωτέρους ἡμᾶς ἐργάσεται. ἐκείνῳ μὲν οὖν ὅτι τὸ τοιοῦτον γυμνάσιον ἀπορωτέρους ἡμᾶς ἐργάζεται μᾶλλον ἤπερ εὐπορωτέρους, ἐπειράθην ἐνδείξασθαι τότε· νυνὶ δὲ τοῖς ἄλλοις ἐπιδείξω προδιηγησάμενος, ἥ τίς ποτ’ ἐστὶν ἡ χρεία τῆς τοιαύτης θεωρίας.

RENDERING 1

At that time, hearing it for the first time, I marvelled at those who propounded the problem in roughly this way: if someone is in paroxysm for five hours and is remitted for two, so that the whole [479] period is of seven hours, and this happens in succession, what type must one consider it to be? I answered him at once that this cannot even occur. For paroxysms of so short a duration make their declines long, and for the most part even arrive at apyrexia. "But you," I said, "describe a paroxysm belonging to a most mild disease, and then by the continuity of the periods you declare it in turn most deadly." "Do not consider at all for the present," he said, "whether the hypothesis is impossible or possible, but allow such things to be investigated for the sake of exercise. For it will make us better equipped for the discovery of the varied types." To him, then, I tried at the time to show that such an exercise makes us more at a loss rather than better equipped; but now I shall demonstrate it to the others, having first explained what the use of such a theory actually is.

RENDERING 2

It was at this time that I first heard, and marvelled at, those who propose problems in roughly this way: if someone, after a paroxysm lasting five hours, has a remission for two, so that the whole [479] period is of seven hours, and this happens in unbroken succession, what must one take the type to be? I answered him at once that this cannot even happen. For paroxysms of such short duration make their declines long, and for the most part they indeed even reach freedom from fever. 'You,' I said, 'describe a paroxysm of a disease that is most mild, but by the continuity of its periods you then show the same disease to be most deadly.' 'For the present at least,' he said, 'do not consider at all whether the hypothesis is impossible or possible, but allow such questions to be investigated for the sake of exercise. For it will make us better equipped for the discovery of the varied types.' To him, then, I tried at that time to show that exercise of this kind makes us more at a loss rather than better equipped. Now I shall demonstrate it to the others, after first setting out what the use of such a theoretical inquiry is.

ἀεὶ τοίνυν εὑρεθήσεταί σοι τὸ μὲν εἶδος τῶν τύπων ἐκ τῶν ἐν τῷ διαγράμματι γεγραμμένων [496] ἀριθμῶν· τὸ δὲ πλῆθος αὐτῶν ἐκ τοῦ γνῶναι πόστον ἐστὶ τὸ τοῦ ἀριθμοῦ μέρος ὁ τῶν κατὰ τοὺς παροξυσμοὺς ὡρῶν ἀριθμὸς, ὅπερ ταὐτόν ἐστι τῷ γνῶναι κατὰ τίνα τῶν ἀριθμῶν ὁ κατὰ τοὺς παροξυσμοὺς μετρεῖ τὸν τοῦ τύπου· παρονομάζονται γὰρ ἀλλήλοις ὅ τ’ ἀριθμὸς οὗτος καὶ τὸ μόριον. οὐδὲν γοῦν διαφέρει τρίτον εἶναι μέρος φάναι τῶν ιβ΄ τὸν τῶν δ΄ ἀριθμὸν, ἢ μετρεῖσθαι πρὸς τοὺς τέσσαρας τὸν δώδεκα κατὰ τὸν τρία. ταὐτὸν δέ ἐστι τούτῳ κᾂν οὕτως εἴπωμεν, ὁ τέσσαρα τὸν δώδεκα μετρεῖ κατὰ τὸν τρία. γράψας οὖν πάλιν ἐν κεφαλαίοις βραχέσι τὴν μέθοδον, ἐφ’ ἕτερον ἐφεξῆς μεταβήσομαι λόγον. ἔσονται δ’ ὥσπέρ τινες αὐτῆς ἀφορισμοὶ τοιοίδε. ἐπισκέπτεσθαι χρὴ τῶν κατὰ τοὺς παροξυσμοὺς περιόδων τὰς ὥρας, πόστον μέρος εἰσὶ τῶν κατὰ τοὺς τύπους ὡρῶν. ὁ μὲν γὰρ τῷ μέρει παρονομαζόμενος ἀριθμὸς τὸ πλῆθος ἔσται τῶν τύπων· ὁ δ’ ὑπὸ τούτων μετρούμενος αὐτὸς ὁ τύπος.

RENDERING 1

Always, therefore, the species of the types will be found by you from the numbers written in the diagram; [496] and their number (multitude) from knowing what fraction of the number the number of the hours at the paroxysms is, which is the same as knowing according to which of the numbers the number at the paroxysms measures that of the type; for this number and the part are named after one another. At any rate, it makes no difference whether one says that the number four is a third part of twelve, or that twelve is measured by four according to three. And it is the same as this even if we put it thus: four measures twelve according to three. Having therefore written out the method again under brief heads, I shall pass next to another discussion. And its aphorisms, as it were, will be of the following kind. One must examine the hours of the periods at the paroxysms, what fraction they are of the hours of the types. For the number named after the part will be the number (multitude) of the types; and the number measured by these is the type itself.

RENDERING 2

You will therefore always find the kind of the types from the numbers written in the diagram [496], and their number from knowing what fraction of the number the number of the hours of the paroxysms is. This is the same as knowing according to which of the numbers the number of the paroxysms measures that of the type, for this number and the part are named after one another. It makes no difference, at any rate, whether one says that the number 4 is a third part of 12, or that 12 is measured by the four according to the three. And it is the same as this if we say it thus: four measures twelve according to three. Having then once more written out the method in brief headings, I shall pass next to another discussion. Its aphorisms, as it were, will be as follows. One must examine the hours of the periods of the paroxysms, to see what fraction they are of the hours of the types. For the number named after the part will be the number of the types, and the number measured by these hours will be the type itself.

RENDERING 1

For if the fever that has its paroxysm every twenty-three hours is not an anticipating quotidian, it must necessarily be four [501] and twenty twenty-fourth-day fevers; just as, then, the one that is late by one hour must also be twenty-sixth-day fevers, and this too twenty and four of them. Therefore those who suppose that the types extend to fifteen days do not speak the truth. And much more so when they bring a tertian, or a quartan, or indeed a quintan period that is late or early by either two or three hours into interweavings of types, in the same way as the twenty-four twenty-sixth-day fevers. For, to leave aside their fifteen-day period and come to that of the quintan: in this type, if the paroxysm anticipates by one hour, there will come to be twenty-four ninety-sixth-day fevers; and if it is late, again twenty-four ninety-eighth-day fevers. But if they suppose these very things, namely that some already anticipate and are late, they utterly destroy their own hypothesis. So it is necessary for them to suppose that the types are circumscribed by whole days and nights, or else there is nothing about which they are talking nonsense; and once this is laid down, the types will advance to a long number of days, even if they do not wish it.

RENDERING 2

For if the fever that has its paroxysm every twenty-three hours is not an anticipating quotidian, it is necessary that it be twenty-four [501] twenty-fourth-day fevers; just as, likewise, the one that lags by one hour must be twenty-sixth-day fevers, and these too twenty-four in number. They therefore do not speak the truth when they posit that the types extend to fifteen days. And much more so when they reduce a tertian or quartan or indeed quintan period that lags or anticipates by two or three hours to combinations of types, in the same way as the twenty-four twenty-sixth-day fevers. For—leaving aside their period of fifteen days, so as to come to that of the quintan—in the case of this type, if the paroxysm anticipates by one hour, there will come to be twenty-four ninety-sixth-day fevers; and if it lags, again twenty-four ninety-eighth-day fevers. But if they themselves (αὐτὰ) do in fact posit these very things, certain fevers that already anticipate and lag, they utterly abolish their own hypothesis. So that they must necessarily posit that the types are circumscribed by whole days and nights, or else there is no saying what nonsense they are talking; but once this is posited, the types will advance to a large number of days, even if those men do not wish it.
