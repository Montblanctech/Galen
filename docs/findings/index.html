Galen First Drafts

What the drafts say

678 items from the 13 works: people, lost authors and books, Galen's own life and writings, recipes, cases, procedures, prices and measures, and the places where the draft or the Greek itself may be wrong. Each item quotes the draft and links to the passage, with the Greek beside it. The page opens on the highlights; the order, from most to least interesting to a general reader, is a model reader's judgement, not a scholar's.

How this was made: model readers went through every passage of the machine drafts and noted what a researcher would want. A script then checked that every quotation is word for word in the draft and every Greek word is in that passage's Greek. Nothing else was checked. The summaries are the readers' and have not been read by a scholar, and the drafts themselves are unverified. Much of this has been read by specialists in the Greek; what is new is that it can now be read in English. Treat each item as a pointer to the passage, not as a finding to cite. Every item about a lost author or book, or about Galen's own works, was then checked against Fichtner's catalogue of Galen's works (2023) and the standard fragment collections: none turned out to be unknown to scholarship, and each such item says what is known and where. A few are known to scholars only from the very passage shown here. Report a correction.
