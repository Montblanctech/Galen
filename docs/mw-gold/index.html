MW-Gold leaderboard

Sanskrit lemmatizer accuracy on the dictionary-cited stratum: the words Monier-Williams (1899) cites for a headword, relocated into the Digital Corpus of Sanskrit (DCS) and validated by the DCS annotators' lemma or by an independent Pāṇinian generator (vidyut-kosha). The Sanskrit twin of LSJ-Gold and LS-Gold. Version v0 (12946 loci, 2026-11-09). Numbers never change inside a version; a new text or system is a new version.

Read this first. The only human-lemmatized Sanskrit corpus (DCS) is also the training data of the strongest systems. Rows marked trained on DCS are scored on their own training distribution and, for ByT5-Sanskrit, on sentences it was trained on: those figures are consistency with the DCS annotators, not generalisation. The rows not trained on DCS, and the vidyut validation route, are the independent part of the table.

By validation route, by span, by window and by how often Monier-Williams cites the headword

A locus is validated when the DCS lemma of the word equals the headword (dcs), when a span of compound members joins to the headword (dcs_cpd), when a Vedic verb separated from its preverb (tmesis) carries the root of MW's compound verb (dcs_tmesis: DCS lemmatizes the bare root, so DCS-trained systems are expected to miss these), when vidyut-kosha's analyses of the word contain the headword although the DCS lemma differs (vidyut), or when the word itself is the headword, as with MW's adverb entries (surface). Chapter-window loci come from the Vedic prose texts whose DCS chapter is the unit MW cites. The baseline row is the vidyut generator's own first analysis, so its figure on the vidyut route is circular and printed for scale only.

How the loci were made

window = sentences of the cited verse ± 1 verse (chapter for TS/MS/KS/AB/PB/KauśS/GautDhS, flagged); validation dcs (DCS lemma key = headword key) / dcs_cpd (compound span, incl. a/an-, whose members join to the headword or match MW's member list) / vidyut (kosha analyses contain the headword, DCS lemma shares 3 letters) / surface (token = headword, DCS lemma shares 3 letters) / dcs_tmesis (Vedic tmesis: verb token whose root + nearby separate preverb(s) = headword); exactly one candidate -> gold; several -> the cited verse decides if it holds exactly one; else ambiguous; none -> not_located. Of 27760 Monier-Williams citations into the texts in scope: located 12946, ambiguous 446, not located 11413, cited verse not in our edition 2955. The token is the DCS word (sandhi resolved, compounds split by the annotators); the DCS dump carries no verse numbers, so every sentence was aligned to a verse-numbered open edition first (GRETIL, Hellwig's Vedic Prose Corpus, ambuda-org/dcs); every system was run on the DCS words pre-segmented, so there is no segmentation step in the scoring.

Honesty

Monier-Williams cites what its lexicographers found worth citing: this is the rare, dictionary-worthy stratum, not overall accuracy, and treebank test scores are not comparable with it. Only texts held by the DCS with a verse-numbered open edition are covered; the Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa and Harivaṃśa are absent because MW cites editions whose numbering the critical editions do not share. The verse alignment is a step the Greek and Latin twins did not need and its error enters the gold; the blind check measures the headword, not the passage. The gold lemma is the DCS annotators' lemma, so agreement is bounded by one annotation tradition. No human reader has judged a sheet of these loci yet. Nothing here patches any system's output; every figure traces to a row in data/qc/mwgold/mw_gold_scored.csv.
