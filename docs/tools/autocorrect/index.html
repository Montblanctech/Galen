Galen Density Project

Ancient Greek autocorrect

Paste a line of Greek as a handwriting-recognition model, or a reader, transcribed it from a papyrus: letters in a row, without word breaks. The page splits it into words and proposes the most plausible readings. It points out letters that look misread and letters it cannot explain.

A plausibility check from word counts in public Greek corpora; not an edition; no scholar has reviewed it. It knows which word forms occur and which follow each other, nothing more. It runs in your browser, and what you paste is not sent anywhere.

What it does


    

  • Word division. Papyri are written in scriptio continua, without spaces. The page tries every way of cutting the line into word forms that occur in the counted texts, and scores each cut by how often the words occur and how often each follows the one before it (a bigram language model with absolute discounting).
    • 

  • Corrections, from a small declared list. It may read a letter as another one of the same shape class, the confusions a handwriting model typically makes: Α/Λ/Δ, Ε/Θ/Ο/Σ (lunate sigma, C), Η/Ν and Ι/Τ. It may restore at most one lost letter per word. Each correction costs: about 3% per letter for a confusion, 1% for a lost letter. Any other misreading it cannot undo.
    • 

  • Unknown stretches. Letters that no attested form explains are left as written and marked unknown.
    • 

  • Flags. Each word of the best cut is also checked as written. It is flagged when it is unattested, or when a reading through the same channel fits its neighbours better by more than 4 nats (about 55 times more likely).
    • 



What it is not


    

  • Not an edition and not a reading of the papyrus: it never sees the image, only the letters you give it.
    • 

  • Not reviewed by a papyrologist or any scholar. Its preferences are word frequencies, so it can "correct" a rare but right form toward a common neighbour, and it does not know grammar, metre, the document type or the hand.
    • 

  • Its counts come from literary texts (and Galen), not from documentary papyri, so their spellings and formulae are under-represented.
    • 



How well it does


Tested on 480 lines of 20 to 40 letters from 12 works that were left out of the counts, with HTR-like errors injected at random. The error rates are assumptions, not measured HTR rates. "Line right" means the whole line's words came out exactly. These are the numbers for the trimmed word counts this page uses; the full counts score at most 0.021 better on any of them.

Sources and licences


Word and word-pair counts: the Greek texts of First1KGreek (Open Greek and Latin project), CC BY-SA 4.0, the corpus used across this project, including its Galen texts. 21.6 million words, after 19 works were held out for testing. The page uses 313,788 forms seen at least twice and 1.78 million word pairs seen at least twice; that table (model.bin) is derived from those texts and shared under CC BY-SA 4.0. The scorer is plain counting and five hand-set constants; nothing is trained. Code: MIT. Language model: interpolated absolute discounting (Ney, Essen and Kneser 1994).


The engine is 160 KB of WebAssembly and the counts are 6.6 MB (about 3.5 MB as sent). After the first visit it works offline.
