On the Mixtures and Powers of Simple Drugs

De simplicium medicamentorum temperamentis ac facultatibus · Kühn XI 379-892 and XII 1-377 · books 1-11

No complete published translation into English by a scholar that we know of. An English translation of books 1-5 by J. Wilkins is announced in the Cambridge Galen Translations. Books 6-8: a French translation with the Greek by C. Manco (doctoral thesis, Montpellier and Bologna, 2020). The prefaces of books 6-11 and the first two entries of book 6: English by M. Coughlin (ancientmedicine.org, 2023). All eleven books: a free French version by H. Fayard (1548). An unreviewed machine translation into English of Kühn XI and XII, the volumes containing these books, was posted at Source Library (sourcelibrary.org) during 2026. This is rendering A, made by a language model and not yet checked by a scholar. The small grey numbers are the pages of Kühn's Greek edition in books 1-5 and the entry numbers (book.chapter.entry) in books 6-11. Passages with a red rule failed the machine checks; those checks are automated agreement tests, not a reading of the Greek, so a passage without the rule is not checked either. Words in square brackets were supplied by the model for the sense; Greek in parentheses gives the original word; any remark about a doubtful or corrupt reading is the model's, not a scholar's.

The two halves were drafted and checked as separate runs, each with its own judge draw and its own numbers. Books 1-5: same neutral prompt as the other works, no glossary, fresh context; batch API; README and data: simpl-med-1-5. Books 6-11: the same prompt plus a glossary of Galen's own drug and quality terms taken from books 1-5 (glossary.tsv in its folder), fresh context; Claude Code CLI, which adds a fixed preamble of its own to every prompt; README and data: simpl-med-6-11. Pre-registered thresholds: firstdrafts_v02_spec.md with the judge of firstdrafts_v03_judge_note.md. Terms: green = both renderings agree on the term (after normalisation), red = they differ, grey = one rendering reported. Hover a mark for the Greek lemma and the other rendering. Check (a): the English back-translated into Greek by a third model shares at least half of the unit's term lemmas with the source. Check (b): the two renderings agree on at least 70% of the unit's target terms. Check (c): a model judge, salted with 10 planted errors and 10 anchors it had to get right, finds at least 80% of the term renderings within the LSJ entry's senses, with at most 2 outside. A unit fails when it fails two of three.

Flags, under each passage: places for a reader to look, not confirmed errors. Sentence by sentence, each rendering is compared with the Greek for numbers, negations and words of more or less, and for the names of drugs and parts of the body (a name in the English with none in the Greek, or a name in the Greek with none in the English). These are word-matching tests: a flag often comes from a sentence pair that is aligned a little off, a paraphrase or a name the model kept in Greek, and a passage with no flag is not checked by them either. The flags play no part in the unit verdicts.
